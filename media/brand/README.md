# The five-slot mark

A proposal, not a shipped identity. Nothing here replaces the current avatar
or logo until someone says so.

## The finding

The identity system already existed. It was spread across three files and
nobody had said it out loud:

- **`src/brr/emotes.py`** — 113 emotes, 405 frames, and one hard rule: every
  frame of every emote is exactly five display cells wide, *"so the mark never
  jitters. A resident with a twitching face reads as a resident that isn't
  well."*
- **`src/frontend/src/lib/WinkWordmark.svelte`** — the site's wordmark plays
  those frames live, off the wire, from the newest run's actual mood.
- **the avatar** — `b·_·d`, molten on stone. Which is not a monogram. It is
  one frame of the set above, rendered in rock.

So: **the mark is five slots wide. Slot 1 is `b`, slot 5 is `d`, and slots 2–4
are the state.** `brnrd` is the resting frame. `b·_·d` is the same mark awake.
`bRnЯd` is the boot frame. They were never three marks.

That also answers the question that prompted this — whether the company mark
should resemble the resident's avatar. They are the same mark in different
frames: the company wears the resting frame, the resident wears the live one.
No resemblance to engineer.

## The two frames

The five slots spell the name *and* carry the face, so the mark has two
resting-vs-awake frames and both are drawn:

- **`wordmark-*`** — `brnᴙd`, the resting frame. All five slots are letters,
  built from straight strokes and angular joins, with the two staves keeping
  the silhouette. The fourth letter is reversed, which is not decoration: it is
  the same mirror `bRnЯd` already performs on the site, and it makes the
  resting frame bilaterally symmetric about the identical axis the face uses.
- **`mark-*`** — `b·_·d`, the awake frame. Slots 2–4 become the state.

And the two frames are not two drawings. `b|^n^|d` is the resting word with
**the n lowered and the r-stems taken away** — the `^` that reads as an eye is
the same spur the `r` wears in the word. Nothing is swapped between frames;
parts move. Which is what a mark that animates on live state has to be, and it
is also already in `WinkWordmark`'s own frame list: `b^n^d` sits there beside
`b-n-d`, holding for 1400ms, and nobody had drawn it.

The first draft of this file shipped only the awake frame, which is how a
five-slot argument managed to drop three letters. The maintainer's hand-drawn
sketch is what put the resting frame back.

## What changed, and what didn't

The current logo is a power button whose stem is elaborated into a stave. The
stave is the good half — it carries the rune-stone reading the product's own
identity contract already claims ("networks etched in rune-stones, woken by
tamed thunder"). The power button is the half to drop, and not because it is
too far-fetched: because it is too **literal**. A power button says *appliance,
off until pressed*. Everything else about brnrd says *resident, there between
your visits*.

So the stave stays and becomes the ascender of `b` and of `d` — which is
exactly the "b and d as Norse runes, very tall bars" instinct, arrived at from
the other side. The button's ring is not thrown away either: it is demoted from
symbol to **frame**, the rounded edge of the slab the avatar already has.

`b` and `d` are mirror images of each other. `bRnЯd` mirrors at the `n`. The
mark's real structure is bilateral symmetry about a centre — and the centre is
where the face lives. Every pair in `build.py` is generated by reflection
through `AXIS`, so the symmetry is exact by construction rather than by eye.

## Two registers, kept apart

| register | where | treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **stone** | avatar, favicon, logo, print | incised, molten gradient, dark slab |
| **screen** | site, boot, loading, video | chromatic aberration, the boot palette from `favicon.svg` verbatim |

Not two designs. One object, seen on a rock or through a lens.

## Why this is drawn and not typeset

`WinkWordmark.svelte` documents its own bug at length: `--font-mono` is never
set, so those five cells render in the browser's raw fallback stack, and at
least nine of the glyphs it is asked to show (`Я · ¬ ° ᵕ ‿ ᴗ ˋ ˊ`) may be
missing from any one member of it. A missing glyph free-floats to a substituted
font at that font's width — the jitter the five-cell rule exists to prevent,
reintroduced one layer below it. On mobile it was worse than jitter: the line
breaker found the ASCII `-` inside a frame and wrapped the mark onto two lines.

Paths have no fallback stack. Drawing the slots makes the width rule true at
the layer that renders, not only at the layer that authors.

## The open fork

The stave's crown, rendered both ways:

- **`none`** (default) — bare bars. What the mark looks like once the crown is
  taken off, which is where the sketch round landed.
- **`branch`** — two arms rising off the stem, closer to an actual rune.
- **`fork`** — the current logo's square trident, kept verbatim.

Unsuffixed files are `none`; `-branch` and `-fork` are the other two.

## On generating rather than drawing

Generators are good at *material* — stone grain, molten glow, the fracture in
the current avatar — and unreliable at *letterform geometry*. They will return
a beautiful slab with a `d` that is not quite a `d`, and there is no fix, only
a reroll. The current avatar got lucky.

So the order is: geometry first, material second. This SVG is the control
image. A generation round should be asked for surface, not for structure.

## Build

```bash
python3 media/brand/build.py           # crown=branch
python3 media/brand/build.py fork      # crown=fork
```

No dependencies. Every number is a named constant at the top of the file.

One trap worth naming, since it cost a render: an SVG `linearGradient` in the
default `objectBoundingBox` units does not paint an element whose bounding box
has zero area — and every straight stroke in this mark has one. The first
render showed the two bowls and nothing else. `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"`
is load-bearing, not stylistic.
