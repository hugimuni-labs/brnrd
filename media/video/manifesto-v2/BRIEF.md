Rework this film rather than reinventing it.
The previous brnrd-unbound version had the correct instinct for editing: abrupt substitutions, graphic interruptions, materially different scenes, fast visual ideas and unexpected transitions. Its problem was that many individual assets and compositions looked cheap.
The latest manifesto version solved much of the rendering quality but lost that editing intelligence. After the provider section it becomes a slow cybernetic 3D flythrough. That is specifically what we do not want.
Treat the MIDA reference as the benchmark for motion grammar, not as a look to copy. Notice how it repeatedly changes the representation of an idea—scale, medium, crop, typography, texture, dimensionality, annotation—while the semantic subject remains understandable. It alternates readable holds with bursts of extremely dense 1–6 frame transformations.
Make this the strongest motion-design piece in your reel. Prioritize editorial invention over continuous camera animation.
Keep the strongest parts of the current provider sequence, but substantially compress the film. Aim around 30–45 seconds, not ~90 seconds.
The story remains very simple:
Codex does useful work → continuation is blocked → human manually carries the work into Claude → another isolated agent acts without that continuity → brnrd breaks the session boundary → from then on the same resident/work can inhabit different harnesses, machines and projects.
Do not explain this through long fake chat transcripts. Pick one persistent visual artifact—the branch / PR / task / piece of code—and make us recognize it whenever it reappears.
After brnrd appears, do not settle into a cyber world and fly through it.
The schematic space should itself be unstable and editorial:
• 2D diagram becomes axonometric architecture
• architecture snaps into a terminal
• terminal rows become machine blocks
• blocks reproject as repo topology
• a branch line becomes a physical rail
• the rail turns 90 degrees and becomes a process timeline
• provider environments can momentarily occupy faces or slices of the same structure
• perspective can abruptly rebase or switch axes
• foreground typography can become geometry and vice versa
Think state transformation, not "camera tour."
The resident should not travel as a colorful energy ribbon. Its passage should look like a beautiful fault in the rendering of reality: local coordinate systems misregister, surfaces shear, layers briefly disagree about depth, geometry gets re-indexed, reflections split, typography lands on impossible planes, and extremely thin prismatic diffraction appears at the boundary. Mostly dark/white/system colors; chromatic color is an optical consequence, not a rainbow trail.
brnrd should feel like something that can glitch through representations, not something flying through a virtual city.
Avoid sustained generic wireframe/cyber visuals. If a shot has looked fundamentally the same for ~1.5–2 seconds, transform its visual language, composition, medium or scale. This does not mean constant hard cuts; transformations can occur inside the shot.

---
Follow-up:
Use continuous camera movement sparingly. Camera movement is not a substitute for motion design.
Use:
• hard graphic substitutions
• match cuts
• sudden scale inversions
• 1–6 frame inserts
• typography becoming physical geometry
• changes from flat → dimensional → flat
• masking by foreground objects
• perspective reprojection
• print/raster/vector/material switches
• abrupt changes of negative space
• annotations that appear only for a few frames
• occasional deliberately ugly/raw frames surrounded by pristine work
Preserve moments of stillness. Density should come in bursts.
Make every major transition contain an idea. Avoid transitions whose entire idea is "move the camera to the next thing."
Most importantly: take creative freedom. The above is a direction, not a shot list. Surprise me. If you find a more elegant visual metaphor, use it.
